## Curated videos and resources (ordered)

### 01 — Body Water Compartments & Ionic Composition

2. **[Indicator-Dilution Principle — second example (optional)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RfLQRBytSiY)** — Study More (video). Slides 9–10. A second worked explanation of conservation of mass and calculation of distribution volume; optional if you already understand the first lecture.

## What to check after studying

- 60–40–20 rule is an approximation; calculate volumes for a 70-kg adult, and explain effects of age/adiposity.
- Indicator dilution: V = A/C after equilibration, accounting for tracer loss when applicable; choose D₂O, inulin and albumin markers based on barriers.
- Predict the six Darrow–Yannet states; distinguish changes in total osmolality from effective tonicity.
- Trace RVD (KCl/osmolyte efflux) and RVI (NKCC1/NHE1-dependent solute influx), then acute vs chronic brain adaptation.
- Trace ADH release, collecting-duct V2 → cAMP/PKA → AQP2 insertion, and Loop of Henle countercurrent multiplication.
- Calculate the direction of Starling capillary fluid flux and connect hydrostatic/oncotic changes to edema. **Note**: the lecture uses a classical simplified model; revised Starling/glycocalyx models limit sustained venous reabsorption in many tissues.
- Explain CSF formation and drainage, CSF protection and pH-dependent respiratory signaling, hydrocephalus, and the perivascular glymphatic hypothesis.
