## Key checks before the exam

1. Connect **I = g(Vm − Eion)** to ionic current direction, reversal potential and membrane conductance; distinguish it from circuit Ohm's law.
2. Understand the **Nernst equation** for a single permeant ion versus **Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz** for several ions; calculate and interpret the chord-conductance approximation in slide 50.
3. Explain graded potential decay/summation and all-or-none regenerative action potentials, threshold, absolute/relative refractory periods and accommodation.
4. For Hodgkin–Huxley voltage-clamp experiments, predict how **TTX** isolates delayed outward K+ current and **TEA** blocks K+ currents, leaving transient inward Na+ current.
5. Define **rheobase**, **chronaxie**, **τ = RmCm**, and **λ** (length constant) and explain why decreasing axial resistance or increasing insulation can increase propagation speed.
6. Explain why myelination speeds conduction and how **CNS MS** differs anatomically from **PNS Guillain–Barré syndrome** (GBS has no separately verified directly matched video in this set).
