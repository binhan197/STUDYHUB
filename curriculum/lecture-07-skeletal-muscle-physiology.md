## Study sequence
Start every unit with Khan Academy where a matching foundational video exists. Then use Ninja Nerd for mechanistic integration. In later aging/myokine sections, university animations and peer-reviewed reviews are assigned because neither channel offers sufficient matching coverage.

## Coverage notes and scientific interpretation
- Sections 1–7 emphasize muscle contraction and mechanical physiology. Sections 8–11 expand into exercise physiology, sarcopenia, satellite-cell biology, and cytokine signaling, all of which appear in this lecture's second half.
- Slide 12 contrasts skeletal DHPR–RyR1 mechanical coupling with cardiac Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release; these should not be conflated.
- Slides 17–18: the A-band remains constant during sarcomere shortening, whereas I-band and H-zone shorten.
- Slides 21–24: length–tension and force–velocity are separate relationships. A review/reading is used where a full direct Khan Academy video was not verified.
- Slides 29–31: do not interpret delayed-onset muscle soreness as being caused directly by lactate accumulation.
- Slides 38–42: the research slides emphasize satellite cells (PAX7; self-renewal/differentiation) and age-dependent niche/identity; reading from an updated open access physiology review supplies greater detail.
- Slides 48–52: acute exercise-induced IL-6 and sustained inflammation-associated IL-6 have different source/timing/context; muscle IL-6 may have metabolic or anti-inflammatory roles depending on state.
- The material's printed slide numbering has minor inconsistencies in the last half; the mapping here refers to PDF page positions rather than the internal printed numbers.
- No copyrighted lecture slides, PDF pages, or images are uploaded to this public GitHub repository.

## Questions for revision

1. Identify every band and filament of the sarcomere and predict changes during contraction (Z lines, I band, H zone, A band).
2. Explain the role of CaV1.1 (DHPR), RyR1, troponin C and SERCA in E–C coupling and relaxation; explain why skeletal and cardiac E–C coupling differ.
3. List each myosin cross-bridge state with ATP, ADP, Pi, attachment, power stroke and detachment.
4. Compare twitch, summation, unfused/fused tetanus; how do Ca²⁺ availability and stimulation frequency change tension?
5. Distinguish isometric/isotonic and concentric/eccentric, active/passive length–tension and the inverse force–velocity relationship.
6. Compare phosphocreatine, anaerobic glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation for high-intensity vs endurance muscle activity; distinguish Type I, IIa and IIx fibers.
7. Define motor unit; explain recruitment and firing-frequency contribution to whole-muscle force.
8. Why do aged muscle satellite cells show impaired regeneration? Discuss intrinsic changes and extrinsic niche signaling.
9. Explain skeletal-muscle IL-6 as a myokine and how acute contraction-associated secretion differs from chronic inflammatory IL-6.
