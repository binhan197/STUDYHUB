## Study questions (what, why, how)

1. How do input, integrative, conductive and output regions of neurons correspond to graded potentials, APs and transmitter release?
2. Why are electrical synapses faster than chemical synapses, and how does Otto Loewi's frog-heart experiment support chemical transmission?
3. Which parts of the NMJ are specialized for neurotransmitter release (active zone, presynaptic VGCCs) and for postsynaptic excitation (nAChRs, junctional folds, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels)?
4. Explain each step: motor axon AP → Ca²⁺ entry → synaptotagmin binding → SNARE-mediated fusion → ACh release → AChR activation → EPP → muscle AP.
5. Why is EPP a graded local potential, but the resulting muscle AP all-or-none? What does the EPP reversal potential (approximately 0 mV) mean for Na⁺ and K⁺ currents? Use I_EPP = g_EPP(V_m−E_EPP).
6. How is ACh synthesized by ChAT, loaded into vesicles, hydrolyzed by AChE and recycled through choline uptake?
7. Compare MG (postsynaptic nAChR), LEMS (presynaptic P/Q-type VGCCs), botulinum toxin (presynaptic SNAREs), and curare (postsynaptic nAChR antagonist).

## Precision notes

- Slides list muscle nAChR as (α)₂βγδ; this is the **fetal** subunit arrangement. In adult skeletal muscle, ε generally replaces γ. This is additional external context rather than a claim quoted from the slides.
- The Khan Academy NMJ video contains some imprecise statements about adult skeletal muscle gap junctions / Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release. Use the linked Ninja Nerd skeletal-muscle coupling explanation for the accurate DHPR–RyR1 pathway; ordinary skeletal muscle fibers are not electrically joined in the same manner as cardiomyocytes.
- Loewi's experiment studied vagal cardiac signaling (not a motor endplate) and supports chemical neurotransmission generally.
- SNARE/synaptotagmin animations in the MSU resource are videos embedded in an open neuroscience textbook. Advanced EPP current recordings use freely available technical reading since no suitable direct Khan Academy video was established.
- Ninja Nerd Part 2 primarily concerns **Lecture #07 Skeletal Muscle Physiology**, not the core of Lecture #06, so it is placed as an optional bridge.
- Repeat links in different sections are intentional study checkpoints; until unified progress tracking is built, completion marks are per resource.
